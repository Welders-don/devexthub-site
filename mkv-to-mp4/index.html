Why MKV files won't play

MKV is a container that can hold almost anything: several audio tracks, subtitles, sound in AC-3 or DTS. Phones, TVs, editors and upload forms usually expect MP4 instead. Most MKV files already carry H.264 video inside, so turning them into MP4 is a quick rewrap rather than a slow re-encode, and the picture stays exactly the same.

Questions

How do I convert MKV to MP4?

Drop the MKV file into the converter at the top of this page, pick the audio track if there are several, and press Convert. The MP4 lands in your Downloads; big files are written straight to a folder you choose. Nothing is uploaded.

Does converting MKV to MP4 lose quality?

Not when the video inside is H.264, which most MKV files use: the picture is copied into the MP4 as is, so it stays identical and the conversion takes seconds. HEVC video is re-encoded to H.264 so it plays everywhere, which takes longer.

Why does my MP4 play without sound?

Many MKV files carry AC-3 or DTS audio. Browsers and some phones can't play those, so the video looks silent. Tick “Convert the sound to AAC” before converting when your browser can decode the track; otherwise the sound is kept as is and plays in VLC and on most TVs.

Can I choose which audio track to keep?

Yes. If the MKV has several audio tracks, for example two languages or a commentary, the converter lists them and keeps the one you pick.

Are subtitles kept?

No. Subtitle tracks inside the MKV are not carried over into the MP4.
