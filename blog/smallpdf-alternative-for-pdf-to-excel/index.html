A free Smallpdf alternative for PDF to Excel

Convert PDF to Excel · 4 min read

Smallpdf gets the job done, until you hit the two-free-files-a-day wall or get nudged toward a Pro plan. If all you want is to pull a table out of a PDF into Excel, here is a free alternative that lives in your browser, with no daily cap and no account.

Smallpdf is a solid web suite, but its PDF-to-Excel path has the same shape as most online converters: upload your file, wait, download, and after a couple of conversions you are asked to sign up or pay. For occasional one-off jobs that is fine. For anyone converting statements or reports regularly, the upload step and the daily limit get old fast.

The alternative: a one-click Chrome extension

Convert PDF to Excel is a free Chrome extension that reads the PDF you are already viewing and exports its table as Excel or CSV, without leaving the page.

  1. Add the extension to Chrome and open your PDF.
  2. Click the extension icon. It detects the table and rebuilds it column by column.
  3. Export to Excel (.xlsx) or CSV.
  4. Download an editable file, no upload screen and no daily counter.

How it compares

The two tools solve the same task in different places. A few differences that tend to matter:

  • Daily limit. Smallpdf's free tier stops at two files a day. The extension has no daily file cap for text PDFs.
  • Upload. Smallpdf works by uploading to its servers. The extension reads text PDFs locally in your browser, so the file stays on your machine.
  • Sign-up. No account is needed to convert with the extension.
  • Where it runs. The extension works on the PDF already open in your tab, instead of a separate upload-and-download site.

What it does and does not do

The extension converts PDF tables to Excel or CSV, and goes the other way with Excel to PDF. Text-based PDFs are free; scanned or image-only PDFs use an optional, opt-in AI mode for OCR, covered in our guide on converting a scanned PDF to Excel. It is a focused table converter, not a full PDF suite with merge, sign and compress, so if you need those, a web tool is still the place. For getting data out of a PDF, this is faster.
