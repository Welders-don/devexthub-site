The best video transcript Chrome extension

Transcribe Video to Text · 4 min read

A transcript extension turns whatever is playing in your tab into text you can read, search and keep. Rather than a ranked list that ages badly, here is what actually separates a good one from a frustrating one, plus a free pick that ticks the boxes.

What to look for in a transcript extension

Most of them will produce text of some kind. The differences that matter once you use one every day are these:

  • Any video, not one platform. Plenty of extensions only handle YouTube. A lecture on a course site, a Vimeo recording, a webinar replay or a TikTok should work the same way.
  • Timestamps you can jump from. A wall of text is hard to use. Paragraphs tagged with the time let you find the exact moment again.
  • Real export, not a locked panel. You want Word, TXT or SRT out of it, not text trapped in the extension's own window.
  • No file upload. The video is already playing in the browser. A good extension reads it there instead of asking you to download and re-upload the recording.
  • Free without a sign-up wall. Watch for tools that hand you two transcripts, then ask for an account and a card.

The part people miss: reading a video in another language

On YouTube the transcript comes with a language dropdown. Pick Spanish, Japanese, Portuguese or Hindi and the same lesson comes back in that language, still with timestamps. For a long tutorial in a language you only half follow, that is the difference between rewinding for an hour and reading the whole thing in ten minutes.

Other options

Tactiq is the best known name in this space and is a fair choice if your videos are meeting recordings. Glasp's YouTube Summary extension is popular for pulling a YouTube transcript into a summary. Both are narrower by design: one is built around meetings, the other around YouTube. If you want one extension that covers whatever video happens to be open, a general one that works across sites is the shorter path.
